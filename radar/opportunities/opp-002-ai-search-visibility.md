“Companies will pay to understand whether AI assistants recommend, mention, or ignore their
brand/products.”

## Money signals

Direct money evidence is present. Multiple vendors publish monthly software prices, generally from
$29 to $489/month, with higher custom or enterprise tiers. Agencies publish one-time and recurring
services from $350 to $6,500/month. This proves a paid category and paid substitutes exist. It does
not prove that the vendors have paying customers, renewals, attributable pipeline, or that monitoring
alone is the purchased job.

## What competitors measure

- Brand mentions, recommendations, and citations in AI answers.
- Fixed prompts or buyer questions, answer volume, and tracking over time.
- AI platforms/models: ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Google AI Overviews, and others.
- Competitor comparison and who AI recommends instead.
- Sentiment, visibility/share of voice, and platform-by-platform breakdowns.
- Prompt quotas, projects, regions/languages, alerts, APIs, integrations, and dated answer evidence.
- Some bundle remediation: content, site audits, structured data/entity work, and action plans.

## Who pays, based on observed positioning

- Individual SEO users and solo marketers: low-end self-serve plans such as OtterlyAI Lite.
- Small businesses and marketing teams: mid-tier prompt/project plans from OtterlyAI, Writesonic,
  and Rankability.
- Agencies: multi-project/sub-brand plans, APIs, and agency packages from OtterlyAI, Peec AI, Scrunch,
  and Writesonic; agencies also sell implementation directly.
- Enterprise: custom plans, expanded model coverage, integrations, dedicated support, and strategy
  services from Scrunch, Writesonic, OtterlyAI, and AnswerManiac.

These are vendor target segments, not independently verified payer counts.

## Pain evidence and gaps

The strongest collected pain evidence is commercial framing: agencies sell the problem as “who AI
recommends,” and products offer scores, competitor comparison, citations, prompts, and dated answers.
The evidence set does not yet include an independent cluster of companies reporting AI traffic loss,
budget allocation, or repeated complaints. It also does not establish whether buyers want a monitor,
an optimization service, or a combined system.

## Risks

- Vendor pricing proves a category exists, not market size, retention, or ROI.
- AI answers vary by model, prompt, region, time, and personalization; a stable “rank” may be hard to define.
- The measurement surface is already crowded and converging with established SEO platforms.
- Low-cost monitoring may be a feature, while high-ticket spend may require agency execution and proof.
- API/model access and query costs can compress margins or make results hard to reproduce.
- The current research has no independent acquisition channel, founder-fit evidence, or customer interviews.

## Recommendation

**Watch — Need More Evidence before Build.** The hypothesis has real commercial competition and
observable paid substitutes, so Reject would be too strong. Build is not justified yet because the
research has not shown independent customer pull, renewal/ROI evidence, or a differentiated wedge.

Next research: interview 5–10 SEO/marketing buyers; collect screenshots or public customer stories
with measurable outcomes; test whether users pay for monitoring alone versus implementation; and
identify one narrow segment with a repeatable acquisition channel.
